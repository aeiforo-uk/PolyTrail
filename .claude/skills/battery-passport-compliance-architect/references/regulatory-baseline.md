## 1. Legal stack

### Regulation (EU) 2023/1542

Use Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 as the primary legal anchor for battery passports.

- Published in the Official Journal on 28 July 2023.
- Entered into force on 17 August 2023.
- Applies from 18 February 2024 unless a provision specifies another date.
- Battery passport obligations sit mainly in Articles 77 and 78 and Annex XIII.

### Regulation (EU) 2024/1781

Use the ESPR framework to understand battery passport interoperability and registry obligations.

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 established the broader digital product passport framework.
- It amended Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 so the economic operator placing the battery on the market or putting it into service must upload the unique identifier to the ESPR registry referred to in Article 13(1).

### 2025 amendment to due diligence timing

Use the current consolidated EUR-Lex text for due diligence timing.

- Regulation (EU) 2025/1561 amended Article 48.
- The date for battery due diligence obligations moved from 18 August 2025 to 18 August 2027.
- The Commission guideline date in Article 48(5) moved to 26 July 2026.

## 2. Dates that matter

- `18 February 2024`: general application date for Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 unless otherwise specified.
- `18 August 2024`: Article 17 and Chapter VI apply, with specific exceptions in Article 96.
- `18 August 2025`: several labelling and related obligations are already active; verify article-specific applicability live when drafting implementation plans.
- `18 August 2026`: the Commission must adopt the implementing act on persons with legitimate interest under Article 77(9).
- `18 February 2027`: battery passport becomes mandatory for each LMT battery, each industrial battery above 2 kWh, and each electric vehicle battery placed on the market or put into service.
- `18 August 2027`: due diligence obligations under Article 48 apply under the current consolidated law.

## 3. Core passport obligations

### Scope

From 18 February 2027, the following categories need a battery passport:

- LMT batteries
- Industrial batteries with a capacity greater than 2 kWh
- Electric vehicle batteries

### Data content

The passport must contain:

- Battery-model information
- Individual-battery information
- Information resulting from use of the battery

Use Annex XIII to classify data into public, authority-only, and legitimate-interest access buckets.

### QR and identifier

The passport must be accessible through the QR code required by Article 13(6), and the QR must link to a unique identifier attributed by the economic operator placing the battery on the market.

### Interoperability

Article 78 requires the battery passport to be fully interoperable with other Union digital product passports in technical, semantic, and organizational terms.

## 4. Access-right classes

### Public

Public access covers the Annex XIII point 1 data set.

### Authorities and notified bodies

Notified bodies, market surveillance authorities, and the Commission can access authority-only evidence such as compliance test reports under Annex XIII point 3.

### Legitimate-interest actors

Legitimate-interest actors can access the restricted datasets in Annex XIII points 2 and 4 for purposes tied to repair, repurposing, remanufacturing, residual-value assessment, and recycling. The exact class definitions and download or reuse rights depend on the Article 77(9) implementing act and must be verified live.

## 5. Platform controls implied by law

These controls are not optional design preferences. They fall out of Articles 77 and 78.

- Restrict access, introduction, modification, and update rights according to Annex XIII and the implementing act under Article 77(9).
- Store passport data with the responsible economic operator or an operator acting on its behalf.
- Prevent a hosting or processing operator from selling, reusing, or processing passport data beyond what is necessary for the service it provides.
- Keep the passport available after the responsible economic operator ceases to exist or ceases activity in the Union.
- Ensure authentication, reliability, integrity, security, privacy, and anti-fraud protections.
- Give access free of charge according to the relevant access rights.

## 6. Live-check items

Always verify these before final advice:

- Article 77(9) implementing act on legitimate-interest persons and reuse rights
- Any Annex XIII delegated updates
- Carbon-footprint delegated or implementing acts and related formats
- Harmonized standards or common specifications tied to the DPP framework
- Any new Commission guidance on due diligence or battery passport operation
